Getting started

Welcome to Excel Lab. This platform teaches Microsoft Excel from absolute zero to a professional, office-ready level — completely free, with no sign-up. Here is how to use it.

  1. Open your first lesson. Go to Lesson 1: What Is Excel and Why Learn It? Each lesson takes roughly 10–30 minutes to read.
  2. Download the lesson workbook. At the bottom of every lesson you will find practice files — real .xlsx workbooks with an Exercise sheet for you and a Solution sheet to compare against.
  3. Do the exercises before looking at solutions. The struggle is where learning happens. Compare afterwards, not instead.
  4. Take the quiz. Every lesson ends with a short interactive quiz. Wrong answers show an explanation, so even mistakes teach you something.
  5. Mark the lesson complete. Your progress, quiz scores and level progress appear on your dashboard. Everything is stored privately in your browser.
  6. Come back tomorrow. Consistency beats cramming: 30 focused minutes a day finishes the beginner path in about three weeks.

Open Excel in a split screen. Read the lesson on one half of your screen and type in Excel on the other. Reading without typing is like watching gym videos and expecting muscles.

What you need

  • Any computer with Excel installed — Excel 2016, 2019, 2021 or Microsoft 365 all work. Most lessons are compatible with all of them.
  • A few lessons (dynamic arrays, XLOOKUP, LET/LAMBDA) need Microsoft 365. These are clearly marked in the lesson and on the curriculum page.
  • Power Query is built into Excel 2016 and later; Power Pivot needs the Office edition that includes it.
  • No Excel at all? Try the free Excel web app at office.com with a free Microsoft account — everything through Level 4 works there.

The learning roadmap

Excel Lab is organised into seven levels. Finish them in order — the course deliberately avoids teaching advanced tools before you understand the foundations they rely on.

Suggested schedules

  • Casual (30 min/day): finish the full course in about 10–12 weeks.
  • Serious (1–2 h/day): job-ready by Levels 1–5 in 3–4 weeks; full course in about 5 weeks.
  • Refresher: if you already know basics, skim Level 1 quizzes, then start properly at Level 2 or 3.

Beginner guide (Levels 1–3)

The beginner path takes you from “I have never opened Excel” to someone who can build clean, trustworthy spreadsheets.

Level 1 — Fundamentals

Goal: comfort and confidence. You learn what Excel is, how to move around, enter and format data, copy and fill, and write your first formulas with correct references. Do not skip the absolute vs relative reference lesson — it explains 80% of the formula mistakes beginners make forever after.

Level 2 — Formulas and functions

Goal: real computational skill. Aggregations (SUM, AVERAGE, COUNT), decisions (IF, AND, OR, IFS), safe reporting (IFERROR), conditional aggregation (SUMIF/SUMIFS family), and lookups (VLOOKUP, XLOOKUP, INDEX/MATCH). You also meet the text and date functions that power real office files.

Level 3 — Data management

Goal: clean, structured data. Sorting, filtering, Excel Tables (the single most underused professional feature), validation and dropdowns, conditional formatting, removing duplicates and a full data-cleaning workflow. After this level your files stop being “a sheet someone typed” and become data.

Study tips for this stage

  • Type every formula yourself, at least twice.
  • When a formula gives an error, read the lesson's mistakes section before reaching for help — the error is usually one of the classic ones.
  • Redo the exercises one week later without the lesson open. If you can't, re-read just that lesson's summary.

Intermediate guide (Levels 4–5)

The intermediate path turns spreadsheet skills into analysis and business skills.

Level 4 — Analysis and reporting

You learn to summarise and communicate: choosing the right chart, PivotTables from scratch, grouping dates and values, slicers, KPI reporting, monthly/yearly reporting and dashboard design. This is the level that gets you asked to “fix our monthly report” at work — a very good career moment.

Level 5 — Real-world office applications

The most practical level in the course: build the actual systems offices run on — employee databases, attendance registers, leave trackers, payroll with HRA/PF, inventory with reorder alerts, purchase and sales logs, customer/supplier records, expense and budget trackers, invoices and a simple P&L. These lessons follow real office conventions: one header row, dropdowns for data entry, formula-driven summaries, print-ready layouts.

Money in these lessons. Examples use ₹ (rupees) with Indian payroll conventions such as HRA and PF, but every technique is universal — swap the currency and rules for your own country.

Advanced guide (Levels 6–7)

Level 6 — Advanced Excel

Nested formulas and advanced lookup techniques, then the modern professional toolkit:

  • LET and LAMBDA (Microsoft 365) for readable, reusable formulas.
  • Power Query to import and clean data with repeatable, refreshable steps — this replaces most manual cleaning forever.
  • Power Pivot and DAX to build a real data model and your first measures.
  • Macros and VBA to automate repetitive work, from recording your first macro to a VBA report generator.

Level 7 — Practical projects

Ten complete systems, each built from an exercise file with a finished solution to compare against: Employee Management, Attendance, Payroll Calculator, Inventory, Sales Tracking, Expense Tracker, Invoice Generator, Purchase Management, Sales Dashboard and a Business Performance Dashboard. These double as portfolio pieces — send the dashboards with a job application and you will stand out.

After the course

  • Rebuild one project with your own real data (your household budget, your shop's stock).
  • Learn the reference tables well enough that you stop searching for syntax.
  • Optional next tools that complement Excel: Power BI (same DAX you already started), Google Sheets (same concepts), and SQL (for larger data).

Project instructions

Every Level 7 project follows the same rhythm. Use it — it is how professional spreadsheet work is done.

  1. Read “What You Will Build”. Understand the system's purpose and its sheets before touching Excel.
  2. Download the exercise file. It contains raw data and empty structures; the solution file exists but do not open it yet.
  3. Build section by section. Each build section gives exact steps, target layouts (rendered sheet views) and the formulas to write.
  4. Test with real cases. Change a value in the data and confirm every summary updates. A system that only works on its sample data is not finished.
  5. Level up. Each project has extension exercises — adding charts, alerts or automation. These stretch exercises are what make the system yours.
  6. Compare with the solution. Not to copy, but to review: look for simpler formulas, better formatting and validation you missed. Differences are discussion points, not failures.

Finish all ten and you will have a personal library of ready-to-adapt office systems — most learners reuse two or three of them at their jobs within months.

Frequently asked questions

Is Excel Lab really free?

Yes. Every lesson, workbook, quiz and project is free, with no ads or accounts. The platform is a static website, so it can be hosted anywhere — including GitHub Pages — at no cost.

Do I need to buy Excel?

Most lessons work on any recent Excel (2016 or later), and everything up to Level 4 also works in the free Excel web app at office.com. A few modern functions (XLOOKUP, dynamic arrays, LET/LAMBDA) need Microsoft 365 or Excel 2021+; those lessons say so clearly and offer older-version alternatives.

I have never used Excel. Can I really start here?

Yes — that is exactly who Level 1 is written for. The first lessons explain what a cell is, how to type, how to save. Move at your own pace; each lesson stands on the ones before it.

How is this different from watching YouTube tutorials?

Structure and practice. Videos are linear and passive; Excel Lab is a progressive curriculum where every concept is taught, applied in a downloadable workbook, tested with a quiz, and later reused in bigger systems. You build a coherent skill instead of collecting tips.

Are the downloadable files safe?

Yes. They are plain .xlsx workbooks (no macros) generated by an open script from this repository. The only exception is Level 6's VBA teaching, where you paste code into your own file — the code is short, commented and shown in full in the lesson.

My progress disappeared. What happened?

Progress lives in your browser's local storage. It survives restarts, but clearing browsing data (or using private/incognito mode, or a different browser) resets it. Your downloaded workbooks are unaffected.

The quiz says I got a low score. What now?

Re-read the lesson sections the wrong answers point to, redo one exercise, and retake the quiz (the Reset button). Anything above 80% before moving on is a good bar for new material.

Does Excel Lab teach macros and VBA safely?

The lessons teach you to create macros in your own files and explain macro security: only enable macros in workbooks you trust, and remember Excel cannot store macros in a normal .xlsx — you will save as .xlsm when following Level 6.

Can I use Excel Lab offline?

Once the site has loaded, lessons render from files already downloaded, so the pages keep working offline in the same tab. The downloadable workbooks obviously work offline forever.

Who writes the lessons?

The platform and its content are maintained in the open at github.com/akhil-tg/Excel. Every formula and quiz answer is checked against Excel's actual behaviour, and improvements ship as simple file edits.
